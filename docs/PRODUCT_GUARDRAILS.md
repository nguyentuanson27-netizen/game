# Product Guardrails

## Purpose

Keep the game recognizable and grounded without becoming a thinly disguised simulation of a real company or real scandal.

## Fictionalization rule

> Real-world systems, fictional world.

The game may borrow generic marketplace dynamics such as rider incentives, merchant fees, surge pricing, PR crises, competition and regulation. It must not recreate identifiable real-world companies, executives or controversies 1:1.

## Brand and identity distance

Do not:

- use real company names, logos or near-copy names;
- imitate distinctive brand colours, typography, icons, slogans or app layouts closely enough to imply affiliation;
- use service names that are obvious near-copies of real products;
- create fictional executives that clearly map to real individuals;
- reproduce a recognizable real scandal and add invented criminal or unethical allegations.

Use:

- fictional city and institutions;
- fictional companies and competitors;
- original visual identity;
- transformed, composite business situations;
- generic industry mechanics rather than copied narratives.

## Product differentiation

The product must not rely on "ride-hailing company but with bicycles" as its only distinction.

Its intended differentiators are:

1. precedent and world memory;
2. delayed consequences linked to player history;
3. stakeholder conflicts rather than a single happiness score;
4. decision options that evolve based on relationships and company history;
5. growth from personal startup decisions into organizational and power problems;
6. recurring characters and institutions that remember the player's treatment of them.

## No morality meter

Do not reduce the game to Good vs Evil.

Clean, pragmatic, grey and dirty approaches should have different costs, benefits and dependencies. The player discovers the company's identity through accumulated behaviour.

## Crime and misconduct abstraction

The fictional world may include corruption, smear campaigns, coercion, blackmail, sabotage, fixers and criminal relationships as narrative/business choices.

Keep these at the level of strategic decisions and consequences. Do not provide procedural guidance for committing real-world wrongdoing.

## Scope guardrails

Prototype should not expand into:

- detailed city builder;
- realtime delivery routing simulator;
- deep stock market simulation;
- deep political simulator;
- multiplayer;
- live-service economy;
- runtime generative-AI storytelling;
- hundreds of individually simulated workers;
- multiple countries or a giant world map.

A new mechanic should be added only if it strengthens the central loop:

`decision -> state/relationship/precedent -> consequence -> new decision`

## Content guardrail

Do not add drama merely because it is dramatic. Ask:

> Why is this happening now, given the player's company state and history?

If there is no good answer, the event should normally be rewritten or removed.

## Mobile guardrail

- Primary interactions must work comfortably in portrait.
- Avoid dense dashboards.
- Normal events should require little or no scrolling.
- Do not hide essential decisions behind precision gestures.
- Keep the main loop usable in 5–15 minute sessions.

## Release/legal review note

These guardrails reduce product risk but are not a substitute for jurisdiction-specific legal review before commercial release, especially for trademark, defamation, store policy, age-rating and local game-publishing requirements.
